The two-way coupling

Every conventional forecast treats weather as an input only. The real atmosphere runs a loop: aerosol blocks sunlight, the surface heats less, the mixed layer grows shallower, and the same emissions end up more concentrated, which blocks more sunlight. This is that loop, solved to convergence and measured against a pristine-atmosphere control.

Photolysis: the stronger pathway

aerosol blocks ultraviolet, so less ozone is made

There are two aerosol mechanisms and they are not equal. Dimming the sun to cool the surface and shallow the mixed layer changes ozone by 1–3%. Blocking the ultraviolet that drives the photochemistry changes it by 10–12%. The second is the one that matters, and it acts on ozone directly rather than through four intermediate steps.

Against published Delhi values

high-aerosol daylight hours

Bounds come from the regime the published figures were measured in: winter haze episodes in daylight. A season-wide average, which includes clean afternoons and nights when the radiative feedback is correctly zero, is a different quantity and is reported separately rather than judged.

Atmospheric inversion tracker

A temperature inversion puts a lid on the city: emissions released under it cannot escape upward, so concentration climbs even when emissions are flat. Delhi's worst winter episodes are inversion episodes.

Why three charts and not one

Inversion strength is in kelvin, mixing depth in metres, ventilation coefficient in m²·s⁻¹. Plotting them on one pair of axes would need two or three y-scales, which lets the author choose the story by choosing the scaling. Three panels sharing one x-axis compare honestly.

Stubble-burning plume transport

Each satellite fire detection is released as a parcel and carried forward on the forecast wind, spreading as it travels. It contributes to Delhi's air only when and where it actually arrives, and only if the inversion lid lets it reach the ground.
